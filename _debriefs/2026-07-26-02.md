## Decisions

- **The shared state checkout is shared by design; drive it in place.** The earlier instruction to migrate it was withdrawn. Stage only the entity files you own, never a bare `git add -A` or bare commit, retry once on `index.lock` contention.
- **b4 cut from the sprint.** Its premise collapsed: the canonical selector form has zero adoption across 32 real mappings written as recently as that day, and the target app carries `aria-label` on 2.8% of components, so the migration would demand a form the app cannot support.
- **Scope questions the captain delegated** (xn's cut priority, non-goals, and pre-mortem) are FO-authored and marked as such in the entity, so the gate can overrule them explicitly.
- **Coverage: do not build the ratchet yet.** The adversarial spot-check is stronger evidence than a coverage number, and building tooling before a real executable diff needs it is inventing a problem. Filed as its own entity instead.
- **The cross-model gate is vendor-agnostic.** Preference `codex` → `agy`; no single vendor required, but skipping the second opinion is not an option, and unavailability must be an observed failed run rather than an assumption.
- **RECONFIRM on gz's design reset.** Sunk cost does not enter the decision; cost-to-complete does, and that was three located sites in one file. Two constraints attached: the correction round stays scoped, and any further FO-initiated correction round goes to the captain first.
- **Split gz: ship the structured channel without a `code` enum.** `e2e-schema-contract` owns the vocabulary; a follow-up adds `code`. This dissolved a three-way sequencing conflict rather than picking a winner.
- **EM holds the gate, and may approve in the captain's place.** Gate verdicts route through the EM seat; the FO assembles the review and holds nothing adjudicative.
- **Dispatch to the mini using codex workers, to balance token consumption.** Every stage of 5v and most of 3t ran on a third quota pool.
- **On the hatch-only finding: return to the gate rather than push with a recorded residual.** The gate ruled it a defect and blocked the merge.

## Observations

- The captain's standing goal for this pipeline is that it consume tokens more efficiently, be more accurate, and be less random. Sprint 1 served accuracy and determinism directly; the token goal is served indirectly, by moving work to a separate quota pool rather than by spending less.
- Rework rounds are not where cost accumulates. 5v took the most gate rounds of the four and cost the Claude pool the least; gz spent roughly 48% of its budget in ideation, and two of those four rounds existed only to correct first-officer errors — scope instability, not flow overhead.
- Moving gate authority off the first officer changed outcomes measurably. The FO's own two gates found nothing; every gate held elsewhere found a real defect before merge. The review's attention is set by whoever writes the dispatch note, so its blind spots travel with that seat.
- Reading a diff did not find the consumer-boundary defects. The one that blocked a merge was found by compiling a fixture and reading the generated script, after five reviews had passed the same diff.
- A cross-vendor review whose entire round was discarded for fabricated citations still earned its cost, because the question it had been asked led to the finding.
